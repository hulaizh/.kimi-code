# Editing finance prose

Use this guide for company briefs, investment memos, earnings notes, portfolio and risk commentary, market reports, recurring briefings, and related student assignments.

## Reader and purpose

Identify whether the reader needs a quick update, a comparison, a risk assessment, or a decision-support memo. Preserve the original document's purpose. Do not turn an earnings recap into an investment recommendation or a risk report into a promotional narrative.

Finance readers value a clear hierarchy:

1. What happened or changed?
2. By how much, relative to what?
3. What drove it?
4. What matters next, and what remains uncertain?

Use that hierarchy to remove filler, not to invent a new argument.

## Facts, calculations, estimates, and judgment

Keep these categories distinct:

- **Reported fact:** "Revenue was RMB 12.4 billion for the quarter ended 30 September."
- **Calculation:** "Revenue increased 6.8% year over year, based on the supplied figures."
- **Estimate or forecast:** "The analyst estimates next-year revenue of RMB 54 billion."
- **Interpretation:** "The margin decline suggests that cost pressure persisted."

Do not change one category into another through word choice. "Expected" does not mean "reported", and "rose after the announcement" does not by itself mean "rose because of the announcement."

## Preserve the financial position

Compare the original and rewrite for:

- Buy, sell, hold, overweight, underweight, neutral, or other stance language.
- Target prices, valuation dates, forecast horizons, and scenario probabilities.
- Risk ratings, limits, breaches, hedges, and recommended actions.
- Long/short direction, benchmark, and performance period.
- Conditions attached to a conclusion, such as "if rates remain unchanged".

If a sentence is awkward but changing it could alter the position, make the smallest possible edit and flag the issue.

## Common finance AI tells

### Empty performance language

Before: "The company delivered a robust and impressive quarter, highlighting its strong momentum."

After: "Revenue increased 8% year over year, while operating margin fell 1.2 percentage points."

Use the second version only when those facts already exist in the original or supplied context. Otherwise, retain the claim more cautiously and flag the missing evidence.

### False causality

Before: "The shares fell because investors were disappointed by the outlook."

Possible edit: "The shares fell 3% after the company lowered its outlook. The original draft attributes the move to investor disappointment, but the supplied text does not establish causality."

In a clean rewrite without a separate note, use: "The shares fell 3% after the company lowered its outlook."

### Correlation presented as explanation

Before: "Higher rates caused the portfolio's underperformance."

After: "The portfolio underperformed during the rate-increase period. The draft does not separate the rate effect from sector allocation."

Do not add an attribution analysis that was not supplied.

### Vague risk language

Before: "The portfolio faces significant risks in a complex and uncertain environment."

After: "The main risks are a 22% allocation to one issuer and the portfolio's sensitivity to a parallel rate shock."

Again, use only supplied numbers. Otherwise, shorten the claim and flag that it needs specifics.

### Forecast certainty

Before: "Margins will recover strongly next year."

After: "Management expects margins to recover next year, subject to the cost assumptions in the outlook."

Keep the original forecast horizon and actor. Do not insert a hedge that reverses the intended stance.

### Boilerplate recurring commentary

Before: "This week was marked by heightened volatility as markets navigated a complex macro landscape."

After: "Equities fell and bond yields rose over the week. The supplied report links the largest moves to Wednesday's inflation release."

Recurring reports should state what changed since the previous run rather than reuse a generic macro opening.

## Concision by document type

### Investment memo

Preserve the thesis, supporting evidence, valuation, catalysts, risks, and conditions that would change the view. Remove repeated thesis statements and generic company descriptions that do not support the argument.

### Earnings note

Preserve the reporting period, comparison basis, actual-versus-estimate distinctions, guidance, and management commentary. Do not mix quarterly and full-year figures for smoother prose.

### Portfolio commentary

Preserve the period, benchmark, contribution versus attribution terminology, currency, and whether results are gross or net of fees. Prefer specific drivers over claims of "strong performance".

### Risk report

Keep breaches, limits, horizons, confidence levels, scenarios, and missing-data warnings prominent. Do not soften a warning merely to make the report sound calmer.

### Student reflection or assignment

Keep the student's actual experience and reasoning. Do not add personal encounters, lessons, or opinions to make the text sound more human.

## Final fidelity check

Compare the original and rewritten texts for every number, ticker, date, currency, unit, citation, recommendation, and qualification. Check percentage versus percentage-point language carefully. Then ask whether the rewrite makes any causal, predictive, or comparative claim stronger than the original.
