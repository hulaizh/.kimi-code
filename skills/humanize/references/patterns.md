# Editing patterns for finance prose

Use this reference for substantial English edits. These patterns are prompts for
judgment, not a list of banned words. Preserve the writer's position and house
style. Never add facts, sources, numerical magnitudes, causal explanations, or
personal opinions to make a sentence sound more specific.

## What to check

| Pattern | Editing action |
|---|---|
| Inflated importance or promotional language | Keep the supported finding; remove empty emphasis |
| Vague attribution | Keep the actual attribution or flag the missing source |
| Explanatory phrases without evidence | Separate the author's interpretation from the observed fact |
| Generic background paragraphs | Start with the question, event, or finding already in the draft |
| Formulaic challenges and conclusions | Keep concrete limitations or next steps already supplied |
| Repeated transition words | Let the logical relationship carry the sentence |
| Weak verbs and nominalizations | Name the actor and use a direct verb |
| Forced contrasts or three-part lists | Keep only distinctions the source actually makes |
| Synonym cycling | Use consistent company, portfolio, and metric names |
| Overloaded sentences | Split them without dropping conditions or qualifications |
| Repeated summaries | Remove repetition that adds no information |
| Excessive hedging | Reduce duplication while preserving uncertainty |
| Unsupported certainty | Flag the evidence problem without silently changing the forecast |
| Chatbot filler, praise, and unnecessary emojis | Remove them unless they belong to the requested voice |
| Mechanical bolding and inline headings | Retain formatting that helps the intended reader |
| Distracting punctuation | Follow the document's conventions; avoid blanket punctuation bans |

## Faithful examples

All figures below are illustrative text. A rewrite may use only facts present in
the original or supplied context.

### Remove filler, keep the result

Before: It is important to note that revenue increased 8% year over year, which
shows that revenue was higher than in the same quarter last year.

After: Revenue increased 8% year over year.

### Shorten without changing the comparison

Before: The portfolio delivered a return of 4.2% during the quarter, compared
with the benchmark's return of 4.8% over the same period.

After: The portfolio returned 4.2% for the quarter; the benchmark returned 4.8%.

Do not change this into a claim about risk-adjusted performance or attribution.

### Preserve uncertainty and conditions

Before: If the customer pays on schedule, we expect month-end cash of RMB 12 million.

After: We expect month-end cash of RMB 12 million if the customer pays on time.

Keep both the payment condition and the forecast status.

### Keep a source gap visible

Before: The company had a strong quarter and profitability improved substantially.

Possible edit: The company had a strong quarter, with a substantial improvement
in profitability.

Separate note: The draft provides no figures supporting this assessment.

Do not invent a revenue growth rate, margin change, or cost driver. If the reader
needs those details, request evidence or flag the gap.

### Preserve risk and approval status

Before: Approval is conditional on receipt of a guarantee. The guarantee documents
have not yet been reviewed.

After: Approval depends on receiving a guarantee; its documents are still unreviewed.

Do not present the approval as final or the review as completed.

## Numbers, terms, and formatting

Preserve percentage versus percentage-point language, basis points, currencies,
units, signs, periods, source locators, and labels distinguishing actuals from
forecasts. Keep established financial terms, including investment thesis,
statistical significance, and factor exposure, when they are used correctly.

Use lists for parallel information and numbered steps for a real sequence. Do
not flatten a useful executive summary or risk list into dense prose. Keep code,
formulas, identifiers, and citations intact. A missing-data or source limitation
is substantive information, even if its wording resembles a common disclaimer.

After editing, compare the draft and revision for every number, financial stance,
condition, uncertainty, attribution, and unresolved issue. Report analytical
concerns separately from prose edits.
